TIP 3: Untreated rinse water discharges from plating plant
CHANNEL: intake form

Trenholm Plating Works in Cooper's Forge, Ohio runs a chrome and nickel plating operation. I worked there as a line technician from 2019 until I quit in October 2024.

For at least the last three years of my employment, the plant routinely discharged untreated rinse water into Beaver Run Creek through an unmarked outflow pipe behind the eastern wall of Bay 3. I personally witnessed this dozens of times. The discharges typically occurred between 11 PM and 4 AM on weekends, when only a skeleton crew was on site and the plant manager, Doug Renner, was off the property.

I am not a chemist, but the rinse water contained the standard byproducts of our process: chromium (some hexavalent), nickel, and the surfactants from our cleaning baths. The volume per discharge was substantial — Bay 3 alone produces several hundred gallons of rinse water per shift.

Renner instructed three of us by name in 2022 to "manage the holding tanks" on overtime shifts, which everyone understood to mean opening the bypass valve. I was one of the three. The other two still work there. I am not naming them because I do not want to expose them, but I will discuss this with an investigator under conditions that protect their identities.

I left Trenholm because of an unrelated injury, not because of this. I am not in litigation with the company. I have copies of three internal logs from 2023 that document discrepancies between rinse water generated and rinse water sent to the licensed treatment vendor. The gap is consistent with what I observed.

Beaver Run Creek flows into the Maumee watershed approximately fourteen miles downstream.
