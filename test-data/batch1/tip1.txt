TIP 1: Procurement bid-steering at municipal water authority
CHANNEL: secure drop

I work in the procurement office of the Westmark County Water Authority. I joined this office in March 2022 as a contracts analyst. Over the four years I've been here, I have watched what I now believe to be a sustained pattern of bid-steering by Deputy Procurement Director Marcus Tilden in favor of an engineering firm called Halverson & Pike.

Tilden was promoted to Deputy Director in January 2023, replacing Linda Choe, who retired after twenty-one years. The pattern began with the Northbridge filtration upgrade, signed in November 2022 — one of his first acts in the new role and the first of what would become a consistent series of awards to Halverson & Pike.

In total, Halverson & Pike has received seven contracts from this office since March 2022, totaling approximately $4.8 million. The four largest were:

Riverside pump station retrofit ($2.1M, awarded March 2023)
Hollow Creek main replacement ($1.4M, October 2023)
Eastside meter modernization ($1.1M, July 2024)
Beaumont reservoir liner ($900K, February 2025)

In each of these, the technical scoring rubric was modified after the RFP closed, in ways that consistently disadvantaged the lower-priced bidder. I have copies of three of the original and revised rubrics. The revisions are signed by Tilden alone — under our procedures they should require a second signature from the Compliance Officer, Janet Mwangi.

Tilden and one of Halverson & Pike's principals, Devin Pike, are members of the same private fishing club on Lake Westmark. I have photos from a club newsletter showing them at the 2024 spring outing together. I am not alleging this is illegal on its face, but combined with the bidding pattern, I believe it warrants outside investigation.

I can be reached through this channel only. Please do not contact the Authority or anyone in my chain of supervision.
