TIP 5: Kickbacks on state DOT resurfacing change orders
CHANNEL: intake form

This concerns Lyle Ashbury, the District 6 contracting supervisor for the State Department of Transportation, and Marcellus Paving Company, the asphalt and paving contractor that has won the majority of District 6's resurfacing contracts since 2021.

I worked at Marcellus from 2019 through January 2025 as the senior bookkeeper, reporting directly to the CFO, Roy Tannehill. I am writing now because I left the company believing I had walked away from the situation, but I have come to think that walking away wasn't enough.

The arrangement, as I understood it, was straightforward. Marcellus would inflate the line-item costs on certain change orders — usually traffic control and night-shift labor — by a margin of roughly 8 to 12 percent. Ashbury would approve the change orders without his usual scrutiny. In exchange, Tannehill would deliver an envelope of cash to Ashbury, typically at the Charter House, a steakhouse off Route 19, on the last Friday of each month. The amount averaged $4,000 to $6,000 per envelope across the period I am describing, which was roughly 2022 through late 2024.

I have audio recordings of three of these arrangements being discussed. The first is from May 14, 2024, when Ashbury called Tannehill's desk phone at 3:47 PM and demanded a 6% increase on his cut for the upcoming I-94 resurfacing job. There was no recording system on the CFO's phone, but I was at my desk in the adjacent office and could hear the call clearly through the wall. I documented the entire conversation in my notebook, including the specific dollar amounts and the date Ashbury named for the next handoff.

I also have copies of seventeen change orders showing the inflated line items, which I retained when I left the company. The corresponding state-side documentation — Ashbury's approval signatures on the change orders — is held by the DOT and would be straightforward to obtain through a records request.

I am willing to provide my notebook, the change order copies, and a sworn statement. I will not testify publicly without protection. Tannehill is litigious and Ashbury, I have been told, has friends in state law enforcement.
