# Pitch — Demo 2: Internal Contradictions

## What you're showing

Three submissions that look broadly similar at a glance — long, detailed,
named individuals, specific dates, retained documents. Two of them have
internal contradictions a careful reporter would catch on a re-read. One
of them looks like it has contradictions but actually doesn't.

## What to say

> "Real whistleblowers misremember dates. They round numbers. They
> sometimes describe their access with more confidence than they have.
> An editor on a tight deadline might miss it. The system doesn't.
>
> [Open tip 1.] This person says they joined Pier 7 in March 2023.
> Then they say they personally attended a rate-setting meeting in
> November 2022. Both can't be true. The system flagged it.
>
> [Open tip 2.] Senior network engineer. Then they tell you they
> read the CFO's confidential board memo. And they spell the CISO's
> name two different ways in the same document. Flagged.
>
> [Open tip 3.] This one looks the same. Hospital tip, fiscal year
> mixed with calendar quarter, two different vial counts in two
> places. But read carefully — the source explains both. The fiscal
> mapping is right there. The two vial counts are different things,
> and the source tells you so. The system did not flag this one.
>
> That's the difference between pattern-matching and reading. Real
> tips have apparent inconsistencies that explain themselves. We
> can't have a system that throws those out."

## Why this matters for investigators

A consistency check that flags every fiscal-vs-calendar mention would
reject most legitimate corporate whistleblowers. A check that misses
real contradictions wastes editor time on tips that fall apart on
verification. Demo 2 shows the system does both jobs — flags the real
ones, lets the apparent ones through.
