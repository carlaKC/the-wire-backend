TIP 3: Stent selection pressure during cardiology fellowship at academic heart center
CHANNEL: intake form
RECEIVED: 05 May 2026

I am a third-year interventional cardiology fellow at Mercer Heart Institute, where I have trained since 2023. I am submitting this anonymously because the cardiology training community in this country is small enough that I would be identifiable to anyone who knows the program, and any retaliation would end my career before it has begun.

I am writing about what I have observed during my training regarding stent selection in the cath lab and the relationship between my attending physicians and Lattimer Medical, the company whose stents are used in approximately three quarters of our cases.

The first thing I noticed when I started fellowship was that we use Lattimer stents almost exclusively. In the device-selection portion of our case-planning conferences, alternative manufacturers' products are mentioned occasionally but rarely chosen. When I asked one of my attendings — Dr. R. Vehring, in my first month on the rotation — why we chose Lattimer for a particular complex bifurcation case where I had read about a competitor's product showing better data, Dr. Vehring told me: "we use Lattimer here, you'll learn the platform and you'll be good at it." He did not engage with the comparative data point.

In the cath lab break room there is a pinboard where the attendings post the Open Payments disclosure forms they are required to make available. I have read these forms. Five of the seven attendings I have rotated under in my fellowship have annual Lattimer payments above $250,000 in the most recent disclosure period. One — I will not name them — has a 2024 disclosed payment total of more than half a million dollars.

The "speaker training" sessions that the fellowship is sometimes invited to as observers are dinners at restaurants downtown. The clinical content portion of these sessions is short. When clinical content is delivered, it is usually a Lattimer product slide deck presented by a Lattimer field educator, with one of our attendings hosting and moderating but not actually presenting original work. I have attended four such dinners during my training to date.

I want to be careful about what I am alleging and what I am not. I cannot tell you that any individual case in my training was managed worse because a Lattimer product was used. The Lattimer products are FDA-approved and clinically appropriate for many of our cases. What I can say is that the selection of device in our cath lab is not driven by the kind of evidence-based, case-specific reasoning I was taught to expect during fellowship. The pattern of compensation to my attendings is large enough that I have come to believe it is the actual operating consideration.

I do not have documents to provide beyond what I have personally observed. I cannot share patient records and would not. I am willing to speak with a reporter or investigator if I can do so under conditions that do not identify me to my program. I am also willing, if necessary, to redirect any inquiry to one of my co-fellows who has expressed similar concerns.
