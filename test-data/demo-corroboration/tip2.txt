TIP 2: Lost competitive bid at major academic heart center despite superior data
CHANNEL: secure drop
RECEIVED: 03 May 2026

I am a senior account executive for Vorian Medical, a publicly traded medical device manufacturer that competes in the coronary stent market. I have been with Vorian since 2018. I cover a midwestern territory that includes Mercer Heart Institute, an academic cardiac center I have called on without successfully winning the account for over five years.

I am submitting this because what I have observed at Mercer over the past three years has crossed, in my view, from a competitive disadvantage we should be able to overcome with data and price into something that I believe is corruptly determined. I want to be clear that I am writing this as someone with an obvious commercial motive, but the underlying facts are documented and I am willing to share them.

In April 2024 Mercer ran its standard biennial value-analysis review for its drug-eluting stent contract. Vorian submitted a competitive proposal. Our offering had three documented advantages over Lattimer Medical's incumbent product:

- Pricing: 22 percent lower per-unit than Lattimer's then-current contracted price, with a no-cost on-shelf consignment program that further reduced Mercer's working-capital requirement.
- Outcomes: published 5-year MACE rates from a randomized trial of approximately 8,400 patients showed Vorian's product non-inferior at 12 months and superior at 60 months on the composite endpoint, a result Lattimer has no comparable head-to-head data to refute.
- Service: two regional clinical specialists with over 20 years of cath lab experience, available on-site for procedural support.

Mercer's value-analysis committee reviewed the proposal in May 2024. The committee's recommendation, which I obtained through a contact who later left Mercer for an unrelated reason, was to "transition a portion of the stent volume to Vorian on a phased basis subject to outcomes monitoring."

Mercer's CFO and Chair of Cardiology overrode the value-analysis recommendation. The contract was renewed with Lattimer at the existing pricing tier. The reason given to me by Mercer's purchasing director, on a 12 June 2024 phone call I documented in my CRM, was: "your data doesn't matter, we have a clinical relationship with Lattimer that this institution is not going to walk away from over a price difference." Those are the words on my CRM contact note.

In the following 18 months I have continued to call on Mercer interventionalists. I have personally watched Lattimer reps deliver lunch trays to the cath lab break room on the same days that Mercer attendings are returning from "speaking engagements" Lattimer paid them for. The pattern is observable and not hidden.

I am not in a position to allege fraud — I do not have access to Lattimer's payment records or to Mercer's internal procurement deliberations. What I can say is that the customary considerations of price, outcomes data, and service did not control this decision, and that the publicly disclosable Open Payments data shows Lattimer paying eleven Mercer interventionalists very large amounts of money during the same period.

I will identify myself to a reporter or investigator under conditions that protect my employment. I have my CRM notes, the Vorian proposal, the Open Payments lookups I ran, and a contemporaneous email from my regional manager acknowledging the loss. Vorian leadership is aware I am pursuing this externally and has not asked me to stand down, but they also have not joined the disclosure.
