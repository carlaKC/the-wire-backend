# Pitch — Demo 3: Corroboration

## What you're showing

Three tips, three sources who don't know each other, three completely
different vantage points on what turns out to be the same scheme. A
medical device company paying interventional cardiologists at a major
heart center large amounts of money in the form of speaker fees,
consulting fees, and research grants — and getting institutional
purchasing preference in return.

## What to say

> "These three tips arrived independently. The senders don't know each
> other. Look who they are.
>
> [Show tip 1.] A compliance officer inside the manufacturer.
> She sees the payments going out and the deliverables not coming back.
>
> [Show tip 2.] A sales rep at the competing company. He has a
> better price, better outcomes, and lost the contract anyway. The
> hospital purchasing director told him the decision wasn't about
> data.
>
> [Show tip 3.] A cardiology fellow training at the heart
> center. She watches her attendings choose this company's product
> for cases she would have used something else for, and she's read
> the Open Payments disclosures on the break room pinboard.
>
> Three sources. Three jobs. None of them know each other. The system
> read all three and recognized: same company, same hospital, same
> doctor named in two of them, same dinners described from inside and
> outside. One topic. Three corroborating documents.
>
> An editor reading these tips one at a time over months, mixed in with
> hundreds of others, would have to be lucky to connect them. The system
> connects them automatically."

## Why this matters for investigators

Corroboration is the entire premise of a publishable story. An anonymous
tip on its own doesn't run; an anonymous tip with two independent sources
describing the same facts does. Demo 3 shows the system surfacing the
connection that makes the second source findable — the moment when "I
have a tip" becomes "I have a story."
